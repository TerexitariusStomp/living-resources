1. TECHNOLOGY INNOVATION
====================================================================
Verifiable Ecological Sensing (VES): a distributed sensing pipeline in
which every field reading is content-addressed at capture (SAID),
merged through quorum witnessing across independent nodes, and issued
as a replayable receipt — making provenance a property of the data
artifact itself rather than a claim by the data provider.

The breakthrough: today's monitoring pipelines lose provenance at the
export boundary. Readings become indistinguishable from fabrications the
moment they leave the device; downstream integrity depends entirely on
provider trust. VES binds identity, calibration provenance, quorum
context, and computation into a content-addressed receipt that any
third party can independently replay and verify — integrity that
travels with the data, not the vendor.

A second novel component is replay-verified computation: deterministic
inference over preserved real-organism neural wiring diagrams produces
task traces that independent parties recompute bit-for-bit, giving
self-evidencing computation with no trusted enclave or fraud-proof
machinery. We have demonstrated this across 15 EM-derived connectomes
(302–40,000 neurons) with measured efficiency benchmarks published
under explicit measured/estimated/cited honesty tiers.

The core research question is whether per-reading evidentiary integrity
can survive real field conditions — intermittent mesh backhaul,
heterogeneous low-cost nodes, adversarial readings — at a cost point
($35–45/node) compatible with conservation deployment scale.

2. TECHNICAL OBJECTIVES AND CHALLENGES
====================================================================
Phase I objective: demonstrate that content-addressed, quorum-witnessed
receipts preserve verifiable provenance for ecological readings under
field-realistic conditions at consumer-hardware cost.

Objective 1 — Quorum integrity under partition: characterize receipt
validity as a function of witness count, churn, and network partition
on Reticulum/LXMF store-and-forward backhaul. Measurable outcome: a
formal bound on false-witness admission vs quorum parameters, validated
in a 10-node field pilot.

Objective 2 — Replay-verification cost at scale: measure compute and
bandwidth cost of receipt generation + independent replay per reading;
target <5% overhead on ESP32-S3-class nodes and <1% on the edge.
Existing bench harness (exact op-model + RAPL joule deltas) provides
the measurement method.

Objective 3 — Calibration-to-receipt binding: extend the receipt format
to bind sensor calibration provenance end-to-end, so "verified reading"
implies "verified calibrated reading."

Technical risks: quorum parameterization under real partition
dynamics; digest/replay cost on constrained silicon; calibration-
binding format stability. Mitigations: degraded-quorum receipt tiers,
digest batching, calibration-registry indirection.

3. MARKET OPPORTUNITY
====================================================================
The integrity gap in environmental data is a recognized market
bottleneck. Post-ICVCM/VCMI carbon-market reform, biodiversity-credit
frameworks, and Article 6 all name measurement-verification trust as
the binding constraint on nature finance — a market projected in the
tens of billions. MRV tooling is the acknowledged choke point.

Customers: (1) carbon/biodiversity project developers needing
audit-grade evidence; (2) monitoring vendors needing third-party-
verifiable claims; (3) research and protected-area buyers needing
fleet-scale low-cost sensing. First revenue requires no hardware
capital: verification services and receipts over existing datasets.

Competition: proprietary loggers (AudioMoth-class, $300–1000+) produce
data with no provenance; MRV platforms (aggregate dashboards) verify
nothing at the reading level. No competitor offers witnessed,
replayable per-reading evidence. Scalability: the evidence layer is
hardware-agnostic — existing deployments adopt it without new hardware,
and the $45 universal node multiplies the addressable fleet.
